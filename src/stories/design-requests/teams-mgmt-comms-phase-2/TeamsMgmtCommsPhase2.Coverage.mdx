## P0-1 · Event-level communications toggle

**Requested** — A Hoco can enable or disable each automated STP template per
event. Toggling off stops future sends for that type on that event. Toggle state
is visible to everyone with event access.

**Designed** — A **Communications** card on the event's Registration Settings
tab, between Compliance and Restrictions. One toggle per company-level template.
It is deliberately **toggle-only** — no edit buttons, no per-template config —
with a line pointing to Company Settings › Notifications as the place content is
authored.

**Trimmed after review (2026-08-10)**

- Header shortened from *Teams Management Communications* to **Communications**.
- The line *"New events inherit the company-level on/off state"* removed.
- *Compliance Achieved* dropped from the list and replaced with a second
  reminder tier. What an event needs to switch on and off is reminders, not the
  congratulations notice. (That template was cut from the project entirely on
  2026-08-11.)

**Tiers collapsed to one row (2026-08-12 planning)**

*"At the Event-Level you would not see the different Compliance Reminder tiers,
there would just be one row that represents all tiers and toggles on/off."*

- The *Compliance Reminder - Tier 2* row is gone.
- The remaining row is **Compliance Reminder (All)**. The *(All)* is
  load-bearing: without it the row reads as the base reminder only, and a
  company running four tiers would wonder where the other three went.
- Order is now Welcome → Previously Compliant → Compliance Reminder (All).
- The *Configured with Tiers* story was **deleted** — *"it will just cause
  confusion."* Tiers are still designed and reviewed on *Screens › Notification
  Preferences › Compliance Reminder Tiers*, which is where they are configured.
- **Reminders created at company level no longer arrive as their own rows.**
  Removing the seeded Tier 2 alone would have been half the rule: add a tier on
  Notification Preferences and this card would have grown a *Tier 3* row, which
  is the confusion the decision exists to remove. The card is now **always these
  three rows**, whatever the company built.

This also retires a justification used elsewhere in this document: the decision
to leave the seeded reminder unnumbered was partly argued from the event card
listing *Compliance Reminder* beside *Compliance Reminder - Tier 2*. That pairing
no longer exists. The decision stands on its own — a company running one reminder
should never meet the word "tier" — but it no longer has that corroboration.

**Edge cases covered**

- The card only appears when Compliance Tracking is on, since it is an STP concept.
- A template that is on at company level but off for this event.
- A reminder created on Notification Preferences is folded into the *(All)*
  row rather than appearing separately, and the row's count goes up once it has
  been saved. That count is deliberate: the two screens used to connect visibly
  through a new row appearing here, and folding tiers in would have severed the
  link entirely.

**Find it** — *Screens › Event Registration Settings* → **Configured**.

## P0-2 · Team-level communications opt-out

**Requested** — A checkbox on the team detail page opting a team out of *all*
Teams Management communications. Every team is opted **in** by default.

**Designed** — A single checkbox labelled *Opt out of Teams Management
communications*, in its own **Communications** card sitting between Goals &
Restrictions and Compliance Credits. The card is built exactly like Compliance
Credits — same bordered shell, padding and heading treatment, one control
beneath the heading — so the page reads as a consistent stack of sections rather
than one form with an unrelated control appended to it.

The header is the short form, *Communications*. Alongside *Goals &
Restrictions* and *Compliance Credits* it reads better, and the full product
name is still carried by the checkbox label directly beneath it — that label is
the string that has to be unambiguous, not the header.

A checkbox rather than a toggle, matching the send-checkbox used throughout this
folder. It first sat inside Goals & Restrictions; that card is compliance
overrides and org fields, and a comms opt-out is neither, so it was promoted to
its own section.

**Trimmed after review (2026-08-07)** — the first pass wrapped the checkbox in
explanatory sub-text, an amber warning callout on the checked state, and a
read-only communications-status panel. Review called all of it bloat: *"really
just need checkbox with a label."* The label now carries the meaning on its own.

**Find it** — *Screens › Team Detail* → **Details Notes**.

## P0-3 · Default TM comms (seeded templates)

**Requested** — Every customer starts with three templates — Welcome, Compliance
Reminder, Previously Compliant Notice — each with default settings.

**Designed** — A whole *First-Time Setup* section, because "what a brand-new
customer lands on" is a distinct state worth reviewing on its own. The list shows
the seeded templates and nothing else; each email is also rendered as the team
receives it, with its default configuration beside it.

**Three templates, matching the ticket.** The list showed a fourth,
*Compliance Achieved*, until 2026-08-11: *"please remove the Compliance Achieved
template. We will not be building that in this phase of the project."* Its copy,
its story and its seed entry are all gone, which closes the discrepancy flagged
here previously.

### The subtext under each template

Rewritten 2026-08-11 and now **defined once**, as `TM_DESC` in `_tmc2fixtures.js`.
Two typos in the supplied copy — *"for per team per event"* and *"compliance
reminders emails"* — were left verbatim and flagged rather than silently
corrected; both were confirmed and fixed on 2026-08-13.
It had been restated in three fixtures and four component stories — seven copies
that had already drifted apart, which is why *"update this subtext everywhere you
find it… so the prototypes are consistent"* was a real ask rather than a tidy-up.
Every screen showing one of the three now reads from the same constant.

| Template | Subtext |
| --- | --- |
| **Welcome Email** | Sent once per team per event. Establishes that the event is Stay-to-Play and points the team at the booking link. Intended for teams traveling to the event with a compliance requirement. |
| **Previously Compliant Notice** | Sent when a team that had met its goal drops back below it — usually after a cancellation. Sends once and if they remain non-compliant then compliance reminder emails take over. |
| **Compliance Reminder** | The recurring nudge for teams. Runs on a cadence and date range you set relative to the event start. Intended for non-compliant teams. Add tiers to shift your tone, frequency, or audience as the event draws closer. |

**Tiers 2–4 keep their own escalating copy.** They are not one of the three named
templates, and reusing the Compliance Reminder text would have every added tier
telling you to add tiers. A tier created in the prototype gets a neutral line —
`TM_DESC.addedTier` — in the same vocabulary of cadence, tone and audience.

**A day-one banner was removed** from First-Time Setup in the same pass:
*"Teams Management communications are ready to set up…"* — *"please remove that,
it's unnecessary."*

**Every email is the same eight paragraphs**, enforced by a shared skeleton
rather than by convention:

| # | Slot | Varies by type? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Greeting | No |
| 2 | Situation — why this email exists | **Yes** |
| 3 | Requirement / booked / short by | No |
| 4 | `{{team_group_block_info}}` | Merge field only |
| 5 | Where to book | **Yes** |
| 6 | Cutoff date | **Yes** |
| 7 | `{{noncompliance_policy}}` | Merge field only |
| 8 | Sign-off | No |

Slots 4 and 7 drop out when they resolve to nothing, so a team with no group
block at an event with no policy gets a clean six-paragraph email.

**One source of truth.** The editor's Content field, the preview modal, Default
Emails and First Run all derive from the same `body` array. Change a paragraph
in the fixture and every surface moves together.

**Edge cases covered**

- Nothing configured yet, but the From/Reply address already defaults to Event Manager.
- A paragraph whose only content is an empty variable is **dropped entirely** —
  a blank non-compliance policy leaves no gap in the email.
- *Show merge fields* flips between the sent email and the template source.

**Caveat** — the body copy is **draft**. The spec records all three as "Pending
Default Template Body" and DES-447 assigns the real wording to Scott. The
structure — which variables, in what order — is what is up for review.

**Find it** — *First-Time Setup › Notification Preferences* (**First Run**) and
*First-Time Setup › Default Emails* (three stories).

## P0-4 · Add / delete compliance reminder templates

**Requested** — Users can add more TM Comms templates from the company
notifications page, naming and describing each, and can delete one.

### Renaming, and no toast on add (2026-08-12)

**Change description** is in every row's menu, directly under *Edit template*.
It reopens the add dialog against an existing template, prefilled — the two jobs
ask for the same pair of fields, so a second dialog would have been the first
with a different heading. Confirm reads *Update* rather than *Add Reminder*, and
the intro says what is **not** touched: content, schedule, statuses, recipients.

It applies to **seeded templates too**. Nothing here treats a name as immutable,
and a template's `type` is resolved from its *original* title — so renaming can
never change how a template behaves. On the tiers screen it can rename a tier;
that is safe because `canDeleteTier` compares row **ids** and the next tier
number comes from a **count**, neither of which reads a number out of a title.

Like everything else on these screens it is **staged** — a rename reaches the
list immediately, the shared store and Event Registration Settings only on Save,
and Discard drops it.

**The add toast is gone.** Adding a reminder used to fire a success
notification. The add is staged and the unsaved-changes bar already says so, so
the toast was asserting something had happened when nothing had yet — the same
reason the Send-Email toggle lost its toast earlier.

**Designed — two models, side by side.** Review on 2026-08-10 pivoted this from
unlimited named reminders to a fixed set of numbered tiers, and both stories are
kept so the two can be compared rather than described.

| Story | Model |
| --- | --- |
| **Notification Preferences** | Unlimited reminders, each named in a dialog |
| **Compliance Reminder Tiers** | Up to **4 numbered tiers**, one click each, no dialog |

In the tiers model the button reads *Add Compliance Reminder Tier*, the dialog is
gone entirely, and the button disables at four rather than disappearing.

**The first tier carries no number** (2026-08-11). The seeded template stays
*STP - Compliance Reminder* in every state and additions start at **Tier 2**, so
a company that only ever wants one reminder never meets the word "tier" — and the
ceiling reads as one reminder plus three escalations. That is also how the Event
Registration Settings card lists them (DES-425), so the two screens agree.

**Tiers unwind from the end.** Delete is disabled on every tier but the last,
with a tooltip explaining why. Deleting from the middle would either strand a gap
or renumber the tiers below it under the user. With gaps impossible, adding a
tier is just count-plus-one — the lowest-free-number scan that used to cope with
gaps is gone.

The original model: an *Add Compliance Reminder* action opens a minimal Name +
Description dialog, confirmed with **Add Reminder**. The new template starts as a
**copy of the standard Compliance Reminder** and is marked Custom. Unlimited —
the fixed early/mid/late model is gone.

**Two ways to delete**, both limited to user-added reminders:

| Where | Why both |
| --- | --- |
| Row menu → *Delete template* | Deleting from the list, where you can see the whole set |
| Template editor → **Save ▾** → *Delete template* | You often only decide a reminder is redundant once you are reading it. Making you back out to the list to act on that is a needless round trip |

Both open the same confirmation. Deleting from the editor returns you to the
list, since the template you were editing no longer exists.

**The editor's Save is a split button** (2026-08-11), the same shape as *Edit* on
the list rows: *Save*, *Restore to Default* and *Delete template* under the arrow,
with **Preview email** kept out as its own button. **Restore now warns first** —
it throws away everything the company has written for that email and cannot be
undone, and it is reached from one item down a menu, which is where a mis-click
happens. The dialog names the template, says what is lost, and says what is not
(scheduling, recipients and statuses are untouched). See *Components ›
Notification Preferences › Restore to Default*.

Restoring also does something now: the rich-text field's `default-content` had
been bound to the live content, so *Restore to Default* reset the content to
whatever had just been typed into it. There is a proper snapshot taken when the
template opens.

**Fixed templates sort before reminders.** Welcome and Previously Compliant are a
closed set — exactly one of each, forever. Compliance
Reminders are open-ended. Sorting reminders last, under a quiet
`COMPLIANCE REMINDERS` label, keeps added reminders from landing in among the
fixed templates and making the order look arbitrary. The distinction is
deliberately a small label rather than a heading or a second card.

**Edge cases covered**

- Confirm is disabled until a name is entered.
- Only user-added templates can be deleted; seeded ones can be reverted but not removed.
- Added templates survive a Controls-panel copy edit re-rendering the list.
- Delete asks for confirmation and explains why seeded templates behave differently.
- The group label appears on the first reminder wherever it falls, so it is right
  whether there is one reminder or twenty.

**Trimmed after review (2026-08-07)** — the blurb beside the Add button
("Add as many reminder templates as you need — there is no fixed early / mid /
late limit") is gone. In its place is an **info icon** whose tooltip explains
what the feature is *for* rather than restating that the limit was removed:

> Add additional Compliance Reminder templates to set a specific escalating tone
> or cadence to your communications as your event draws closer. Be sure to set
> the **Days until Event Start to Begin/End Reminders** to avoid overlapping with
> other reminders you have in place.

**Diverges from the written AC** — the ticket says the dialog offers a *type*
choice (Compliance Reminder or General Communication). The newer direction is
unlimited compliance reminders that duplicate an existing one, so there is no type
picker. The AC text is stale, not missed.

**Find it** — *Screens › Notification Preferences*.

## Saving — cross-cutting, both Notification Preferences screens

Not a numbered requirement, but it governs how every setting on these two
screens behaves, so it belongs here.

**Nothing takes effect until Save.** An unsaved-changes bar appears fixed to the
bottom of the viewport the moment anything differs from the last save — a Send
Email checkbox, the From/Reply address, a reminder added or deleted — offering
**Discard** and **Save**.

| | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Adding a reminder | Row appears in the list immediately, but is **staged**. It does not reach Event Registration Settings as an event-level toggle until Save |
| Deleting a reminder | Staged the same way. A reminder that was never saved is simply dropped; a saved one is removed on Save |
| Send Email checkboxes | Held by row id rather than mutating the row, so a Controls-panel edit rebuilding the list cannot lose them |
| Discard | Drops all staging and restores From/Reply to its last-saved value |

The shared session store (`_tmc2store.js`) therefore holds **only saved
templates** — that store is what Event Registration Settings reads, so an
unsaved edit must not be visible there.

**Deliberately not on the template editor.** That view has its own Cancel / Save
in the header, next to the field being edited. Two competing save affordances on
one screen would be worse than either alone.

**No toast on toggling a checkbox.** There used to be one. Once the bar existed
it was also untrue — *"Send email turned off"* claims something happened, when
nothing does until Save. Toasts now fire only on things that actually completed:
adding a reminder, saving, and a real test send.

**Session-scoped, and that is the honest limit.** Storybook keeps one preview
iframe as you move between stories, so a saved template survives navigation to
Event Registration Settings. A hard browser reload clears it. It stands in for
the server; it is not one.

## P0-5 · From/Reply address

**Requested** — A From/Reply setting offering Event Manager, Event Customer
Support Contact, or a custom address.

**Designed** — a single row inside the **Teams Management** section it
configures: the From/Reply select, the custom address field when *Other* is
chosen, and one line of help beneath. It applies to every template in the
section — not per template, and with no event-level override.

Nothing names a concrete mailbox. *Event Manager* and *Event Customer Support
Contact* are per-event roles, so the editor and the email preview report the
role and when it resolves — *Event Manager — resolved per event when the email
sends*. *Other* is a literal address, so that one still resolves normally.

**This replaced an earlier design** (briefly shown as "V1" alongside it) that put
the setting in its own card above every section, and echoed a concrete resolved
address in the editor. Review on 2026-08-07 made three points, and all three are
now the design:

1. *"Too bloated with information"* → card became a single row.
2. *"The preview email that shows below it doesn't make sense b/c the first 2
   selections are relative to the event so you don't know what address any
   particular email would really come from"* → **correct, and the old design was
   wrong.** Naming one mailbox was never truthful, and the email preview
   inherited that false certainty.
3. *"It feels independent of the Teams Management Communications section ... over
   time there will be more sections like Guests, Hotels, and each of those may
   have specific config parameters"* → this drove the structure. The strip is a
   **generic per-section config slot**, not a one-off for From/Reply. Future
   sections fill the same slot instead of each adding another free-floating card
   to the top of the page. It is one shared component
   (`fromAddressSectionStrip`), used by both Notification Preferences screens so
   they cannot drift.

**Edge cases covered**

- Custom address selected but not yet typed.
- *Other* still shows the literal address in the editor, because there it is knowable.

**Resolved 2026-08-10** — the unset state no longer exists. *Event Manager* is
the default and the field cannot be cleared, so there is nothing to warn about
and the asterisk came off with the warning. The one option that still needs
checking is *Other*, whose address is typed rather than resolved from a person;
it is validated for a plausible email.

**Diverges from the written AC** — the ticket says the support-contact option
should respect an event-level override. The newer direction is **no event-level
override**: Event Manager and Support Contact already vary per event, which is
where the flexibility comes from.

**Find it** — *Screens › Notification Preferences*;
*First-Time Setup › Notification Preferences* (unset state).

## P0-6 · Email template variables

**Requested** — 23 merge fields across Event, Team, Compliance, Key Dates and
Hoco Contact groups.

**Designed** — The Personalization menu carries all 23, grouped as specced.
Selecting one inserts the spec's exact `{{event_name}}` snake_case token. Each row
shows the literal token it inserts beneath the human label, because that mapping
is not guessable from a friendly name alone. Search matches label, token and
description.

**Edge cases covered**

- `{{noncompliance_policy}}` is present but flagged as returning nothing until
  P1-3 ships.
- Variables that can return multi-sentence text or nothing at all
  (`{{team_group_block_info}}`, `{{noncompliance_policy}}`) are placed on their own
  paragraph in the default templates so they can vanish without breaking grammar.
- Inline `{{` typing pops the same menu as the toolbar button.

**Find it** — *Components › Personalization Menu* and *Components › Text
Formatter*; in use throughout the template editor.

## P0-7 · Email configuration options

**Requested** — Every template gets compliance-status targeting and recipients.
Compliance reminders additionally get scheduling. The config must be able to hide
scheduling per email type.

**Designed** — A *Notification Settings* panel split into **Audience** (always
shown — Team Recipients, then Compliance Statuses) and **Scheduling** (begin/end
days, recurrence), shown only for `compliance-reminder` templates.

**Revised 2026-08-11.** Everything below came from one review pass:

| Change | Why it was asked for |
| --- | --- |
| *Email Settings* → **Notification Settings** | Matches the section it configures |
| **Audience** now sits above **Scheduling** | Requested. Also reads better for the one-shot emails, where Scheduling is absent and Audience was the only block |
| **Team Recipients** now sits above *Compliance Statuses to Include* | Requested |
| *Recipients* → **Team Recipients** | Names whose recipients these are |
| *Team Manager* → **Housing Contact**; *Group Block Contacts* → **Group Block Contact(s)** | Matches the real roles |
| **Ends** block removed from the custom-recurrence modal | *"There is no end to these."* The begin/end day range already bounds the run, so an end date here was a second, contradictable answer to a settled question |
| **year** removed from the repeat interval | A cadence in years is meaningless against an event a few hundred days out |
| The **Recurring reminder** badge beside the editor title removed | *"Anywhere there is a tag next to the name of the template on the edit page… please remove it."* The Scheduling block already says it recurs, with the actual cadence |

The badge that came off is the second one to go from that spot — *Triggered
email* was removed earlier for labelling the normal case.

**Edge cases covered**

- Welcome and Previously Compliant show no scheduling at all, cleanly — not
  disabled fields.
- Template type is resolved from the template's *original* title, so renaming a
  template in the Controls panel cannot change which sections it gets.
- Custom reminders added via P0-4 inherit the reminder type and get scheduling.

**Find it** — *Screens › Notification Preferences* → open any template.

## P0-8 · Daily job rules & send eligibility

**Requested** — A 6am EST job honouring event, team and config eligibility rules;
one-time send flags; and a maximum of one email per team per day.

**Designed — nothing, deliberately.** P0-8 is engine logic and was never asked
to be mocked. This was stated in the meeting; it was nonetheless treated as a
design requirement and grew four surfaces across the folder. **All four have now
been removed** (2026-08-07), at Scott's request:

| Surface that existed | Where | Removed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Event-level eligibility block (two gates) + the whole **SendingBlocked** story | Event Registration Settings | 2026-08-07 |
| *One email per team per day* note and priority order | Notification Preferences › Notification Settings | 2026-08-07 |
| Read-only *Communications status* block, incl. one-time send flags | Team Detail › Goals & Restrictions | 2026-08-07, with the P0-2 trim |
| Send-eligibility banner above the log | Team Detail › Communications Log | 2026-08-07, with the P0-9 trim |

The supporting fixtures (`EVENT_SEND_GATES`, `EVENT_SEND_GATES_BLOCKED`,
`TEAM_SEND_STATE`, `SEND_PRIORITY`, `SEND_PRIORITY_NOTE`) were deleted with them,
so nothing is left dormant waiting to be re-rendered.

**Worth stating plainly, as a process note:** the rules in this ticket are real
and still govern behaviour — the daily 6am EST job, the event/team/config gates,
the one-time send flags, one email per team per day, the Welcome → Previously
Compliant → Compliance Reminder priority. They just do not need pictures. If a
future conversation decides a Hoco *does* need to see "why isn't this team
getting email", that should start as its own request rather than reappear as a
side effect of another ticket.

**Find it** — nowhere, by design.

## P0-9 · Per-team communication log

**Requested** — A per-team record of every automated communication with
timestamp, email type, recipients and send status, sortable and filterable.

**Designed** — A third tab on the team page: a heading and a table, nothing else.
**View-only** — no resend, no row actions. Recipients are their **own column**,
one address per line, never merged into another column. Status renders as a chip
using the design system's status pattern. Sortable column headers are the only
control on the tab.

**Trimmed after review (2026-08-07)** — review kept the table (*"the table part
of the mock works well"*) and removed everything around it: the sub-text under
the heading, the send-eligibility banner, and the email-type and date-range
filter bar. *"Should be a simple log with sortable headers, that's it."*

**The acceptance criteria are now stale.** DES-433 still reads *"Log is sortable
**and filterable** by email type and date."* Filtering was built to that AC and
then removed by this review. The ACs have been left untouched for Scott to amend
— flagging it here so the gap is not read as an oversight.

**Edge cases covered**

- Multiple recipients on one send.
- Bounced and Read statuses alongside Sent.
- Sorting on *Sent* orders chronologically via a derived date, not the display string.
- Test sends (P1-1) are excluded — this is the record of automated sends only.
- An empty log reads *"No communications have been sent to this team yet."*
  Note this no longer distinguishes *nothing sent yet* from *team opted out* —
  the banner that drew that distinction was part of the trim.

**Open question answered** — the ticket asks whether this should be a general team
activity log or a dedicated communications log. The mock goes **dedicated**: a
general activity feed would mix in reservation and note events for which the
Recipients and Status columns are meaningless, and the driving use case ("prove
this team was emailed") is a communications question. Worth Scott confirming.

**Find it** — *Screens › Team Detail* → **Communications Log** tab.

## P1-1 · Email preview & test send

**Requested** — Preview a rendered email with sample data, and send a test before
enabling it.

**Designed** — The row menu's *Preview* opens the email in a modal, rendered from
the **same shared fragment** as the seeded Default Emails so the two cannot drift,
with *Show merge fields* to flip between sent email and template source. *Send
test email* is reachable from the row menu, the preview modal footer, and the
editor — all opening one address dialog.

**Edge cases covered**

- A user-added reminder previews as the seeded template it was copied from.
- The preview's From line reflects the address actually selected in P0-5.
- Test sends do **not** write to a team's Communications Log, stated at all three
  entry points.

**Resolves a TBD** — the ticket's third AC left "flagged as tests" open. Decided:
excluded entirely, because the log's job is proving what a *team* was sent.

**Find it** — *Screens › Notification Preferences*.

## P1-3 · Event-level non-compliance policy

**Requested** — An optional text area at event level whose content drops into
templates via a merge field, and which is omitted entirely when blank.

**Designed** — A text area at the foot of the Compliance card on Registration
Settings, with hint text explaining it becomes its own paragraph in Teams
Management emails.

**Edge cases covered**

- Blank policy: the paragraph is dropped from the rendered email entirely — no
  empty line, no stray punctuation. Verified in the Default Emails stories.
- Populated: renders as a self-contained paragraph.
- Unblocks `{{noncompliance_policy}}` in P0-6, which returns nothing until this exists.

**Find it** — *Screens › Event Registration Settings* → **Configured** (populated)
and **Default** (empty).

## How this folder is built

Not a requirement, but it is what keeps the screens honest, and it is worth
knowing before reviewing them.

**Both Notification Preferences screens are one implementation.** The configured
screen and First-Time Setup are the same product surface at two moments. The
editor, its dialogs and all their state live in `_tmc2np.js` and are imported by
both, so Edit opens the same editor, Preview shows the same email and a test send
behaves the same way. What stays in each story file is only what genuinely
differs: the list, the starting data, and what "unsaved" means there.

**Components are grouped by the screen they belong to**, mirroring *Screens* in
the sidebar. Each component's docs states what it replaced on the original screen
and, where review changed it, what came out and why — the itemisation lives next
to the component rather than in a list that goes stale.

Components inherited from DES-207 were **deleted** rather than carried along;
they are unchanged there and were never Phase 2 work. Two were kept because they
are: *Personalization Menu* (P0-6's merge-field list) and *Text Formatter*.

## Decisions worth reviewing

Things where the design made a call rather than following the spec literally.

| # | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0-4 | No type picker in the add dialog | Newer direction is unlimited compliance reminders, not a type choice. AC is stale. |
| P0-5 | No event-level override | Event Manager and Support Contact already vary per event. |
| P0-6 | Tokens insert as `{{event_name}}`, not a friendly name | Matches the spec's variable list verbatim; the literal token is shown in the menu so the mapping is visible. |
| P0-9 | Dedicated communications log, not a general activity log | Recipients and Status are meaningless for note and reservation events. |
| P1-1 | Test sends excluded from the log | The log's purpose is proving what a *team* was sent. |
| P0-3 | Draft body copy written rather than left blank | Gives DES-447 something concrete to react to. Not approved copy. |
